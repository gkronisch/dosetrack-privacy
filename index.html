Privacy policy for Dose Track

Effective date: November 14, 2026

Dose Track is a private log of what you take, published by Atlas Keystone LLC (“we”, “us”).

The short version: your log stays on your device. We don’t receive it, and we have no server that could.

What the app stores, and where

Everything you enter is stored on your device:

  • Your entries: the substance, amount, unit, route, date and time, and the optional cost and notes.
  • Substances you add yourself.
  • Your preferences, such as favorites, hidden items and the hour your day starts.

This is kept in a database that is encrypted on your device. The encryption key is held in the secure storage that Android provides for apps. The app will not save anything if it cannot encrypt.

If you set a PIN, the app stores only a scrambled (salted and hashed) form of it, on your device. If you turn on fingerprint or face unlock, Android does the checking. The app never sees or stores your fingerprint or face.

The app also creates a random ID for this installation and attaches it to each entry. It only tells your own devices apart when you use sync. It is not your phone’s hardware ID or an advertising ID.

Your log is excluded from Android’s cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so it does not go to your Google account when your phone backs up.

What we receive

Nothing. Dose Track has no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting, and it contains no advertising or tracking software from other companies. We do not run a server for the app. Unless you turn on sync, the app does not connect to the internet at all.

If you install the app from Google Play, Google handles the download under its own privacy policy. Google may show us overall figures, such as how many people installed the app, and crash reports from people who have chosen to share diagnostics with Google. These never include your log.

When your data can leave your device

Only when you choose one of these.

Sync (optional, off unless you sign in). You can connect the app to a sync server whose address you type in. It is a server that you run, or that someone you chose runs for you. We do not run it and cannot reach it. When sync is on, the app sends that server:

  • the email and password you sign in with;
  • your entries, including cost and notes;
  • substances you added and your preferences;
  • the random installation ID described above.

The connection is always encrypted (HTTPS); the app refuses any other kind of address. The data is not end-to-end encrypted, so whoever runs the server can read it. After you sign in, the app keeps a sign-in token in your device’s secure storage. It does not store your password.

Exports (only when you tap export). You can save a PDF report, a CSV file or a JSON backup. The app creates the file on your device and saves it where you pick. These files are not encrypted, so keep them somewhere private. Where they go after that is up to you.

We don’t share or sell your data

We have nothing to share: your data never reaches us.

Deleting your data

  • One entry. Delete it in the app and it disappears from your log, charts, reports and CSV exports. So that the deletion can reach your other devices, the app keeps a hidden record of that entry, marked as deleted. That record still holds the entry’s details. It stays in the app’s encrypted database, it is included in JSON backups, and it is sent to your sync server if you use one.
  • Everything on this device. Uninstall the app, or open Android Settings > Apps > Dose Track > Storage and choose Clear storage. This erases the log and the encryption key from the device.
  • Data on your sync server. That server is yours, so you delete data there. We cannot do it for you because we have no access to it.
  • Exported files. Delete them wherever you saved them.

Signing out of sync keeps everything on your device and stops sending.

Who the app is for

Dose Track is for adults aged 18 and over. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Not medical advice

Dose Track is a logging tool. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition. It does not give medical advice, recommend doses or tell you what is safe. Consult a healthcare professional for medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and before changing how much you take: stopping some substances suddenly can be dangerous. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects your privacy, we will update this page and its effective date before the change reaches you.
